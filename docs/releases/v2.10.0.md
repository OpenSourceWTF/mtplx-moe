# MTPLX 2.10.0

Qwen 3.8 Flash-Next runs day one as a first-class model with both packs public on Hugging Face. Coding agents get the biggest set of fixes MTPLX has shipped: the tool contract no longer drops tools, cancellations tell the truth, reasoning history is preserved so the session cache stays exact between turns, and the OpenCode and Pi integrations now default to settings measured on real coding work. Long-context decode past 128k improves by up to 54 percent, the engine plans memory for the Mac it is actually on instead of assuming 128 GB, the mlx floor moves to a measurably faster release, and KV cache quantization goes from broken to usable.

## Qwen 3.8 Flash-Next, day one

The 125B-A6B Qwen4-generation preview (GatedDeltaNet hybrid MoE, Qwen Sparse Attention, 32 GB n-gram memory sidecar) is a native MTPLX family from the first day, not a compatibility shim. The in-tree backend is parity-exact against the reference implementation, the model's own MTP draft head runs through the standard speculative lane, and the family got its own kernel campaign before release. Measured through the real server on an M5 Max: about 61 tok/s autoregressive and 63 to 76 tok/s with the native MTP head depending on workload.

- Two packs, public on Hugging Face: [Bare Speed](https://huggingface.co/Youssofal/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-MTPLX-Bare-Speed) (flat 4-bit, quickest) and [Optimized Speed](https://huggingface.co/Youssofal/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-MTPLX-Optimized-Speed) (dynamic 4-bit with 8-bit attention, higher quality). Both appear in the app's model picker, first-run onboarding on machines that fit them, and the CLI catalog, with accurate download sizes pinned to the published repos.
- Both packs resolve the Turbo launch profile by default across `mtplx start`, `mtplx serve`, `mtplx quickstart`, and the app, under the canonical ids `mtplx-flash-next-bare-speed` and `mtplx-flash-next-optimized-speed`. `mtplx pull` accepts both ids and the HF repo names.
- The family kernel campaign is on by default for these serves: a pipelined compiled decode step, GDN input-projection fusion, a single-dispatch GDN decode step, fused conv+SiLU+L2-norm for draft and verify widths, a fused hyper-connection read, and merged MoE gate and up projections. Each carried its own A/B receipt before promotion.
- Layer-owned capture-commit rollback is on by default for the family. Verify rounds dropped from 51.7 ms to 38.8 ms and rejection repair re-forwards went from 8.2 ms per round to zero.
- The 32 GB n-gram sidecar streams from SSD by default, so both packs fit 96 GB and larger Macs with headroom. The memory plan, session bank budget, and the app's Memory Detail card report it as streamed, not wired. Counting it as weights had produced a false "MODEL DOES NOT FIT", a 30 GB smaller context window, and a 30 GB starved session bank on 128 GB machines. A hot-row LRU (`MTPLX_NGRAM_HOT_MB`, default 1024) keeps decode-sized gathers in RAM, byte-identical by test: 5 to 10 percent more AR decode and 7.5 to 16 percent more MTP decode at identical memory, with QD16 prefetch on the streaming path.
- Reasoning effort has full parity with the 27B: the same xhigh, medium, and low levels in the app's picker, `--reasoning-effort` on the CLI, and the `reasoning_effort` request field. The chat default is xhigh. Previously the family silently discarded the field and always thought at maximum.
- Reasoning history is preserved across turns by default, matching the model card and the 27B. The scoped fallback the family first landed on dropped prior think blocks from history, which made every agent round's session-cache commit abort and re-prefill the whole assistant turn. With preserve on, mid-session agent turns cost about 20 new prefill tokens and first tokens arrive in about 0.1 s.
- One numerics note: this family verifies through the generic batched lane for now. The 27B rides its capture-commit verify strategy and measures 6 of 6 token-exact MTP against AR at temperature 0. On Flash-Next a rare near-tie argmax under greedy decoding can differ from single-step decode. It was observed once in 192 tokens in the release gate, and both continuations converged. The speculative acceptance math itself is unchanged and exact at any temperature. The family-native capture verifier that closes the gap is in progress.
- New for every family: `mtplx forge verify --stamp` boots a model on the real serve path, measures it, and writes its runtime contract in place. A family-compatible but unverified folder becomes a verified model without rebuilding or copying anything. Families the tune instrument cannot measure take their contract rows through a locally booted `mtplx serve`.

## Coding agents

MTPLX's job is real coding on your own Mac, inside the tool you already use. This release removes the failure modes that broke that, and tunes the defaults with wall-clock receipts from real multi-file coding tasks driven through OpenCode, Pi, and Hermes.

- The compact tool contract keeps every declared tool (#376, adapting community PR #379 by @ArctifoxNL, with an alignment fix). Over its 1200-character budget the "Declared tools" line was cut at a raw byte boundary, deleting whole tool names at the tail, `task` first in Claude Code-shaped toolsets. The contract's own "never invent an undeclared tool" rule then made the model treat every dropped tool as nonexistent, which killed subagents. Every declared name now survives; only per-tool signature detail is shed.
- Cancellation errors name their real cause (#381). The shared per-request cancel flag is tripped by several unrelated paths, and the terminal frame blamed every non-disconnect trip on `POST /v1/mtplx/cancel`, an endpoint nobody called. The first origin (explicit cancel, client disconnect, stop sequence, tool-call finalization, stall watchdog, teardown) is now recorded and reported in the frame and the metrics.
- Streaming agent turns no longer die with a fabricated "request cancelled via POST /v1/mtplx/cancel" error (#332, #343). After a complete streamed tool call the server ends the turn by cancelling its own generation; when the worker's acknowledgement outran the stream loop's 250 ms poll, the loop misread its own cancel as a foreign one and killed the healthy turn. Tool-calling streams now drain until the worker acknowledges and end with a proper `tool_calls` frame. This hit tool-calling agents hardest on slower machines and longer contexts.
- Unexecuted tool calls leave a visible notice instead of silence (#349). When a client asks about files and the model emits tool markup the server cannot execute, the chat shows a truthful notice naming the tool, the model sees the same notice in history, and `mtplx_stats` counts it. The app persists the same truthful result for tool calls that finish past a chat's tool-round budget.
- OpenCode and Pi configs default to the family's agent-lane reasoning effort, which is medium for Flash-Next. On the identical multi-file coding task the xhigh chat default measured 150.2 s wall clock against 44.2 s at medium, with the same correct output; the extra time was thinking tokens, not code. Chat surfaces keep xhigh, and both clients' effort pickers still offer it per request.
- The Pi integration owns its transport contract on re-sync. A compatibility block written by an older MTPLX (with the effort dial disabled) used to survive every re-sync and silently kept Pi from sending reasoning effort after an upgrade. MTPLX's own compatibility keys now update; user-added keys still survive.
- Agent requests are validated against family truth: depth requests respect the family ceiling (Flash-Next accepts up to 5), and requests adopt the pack-declared sampler instead of a generic temperature 0.6 that was never the family's law.
- Session-cache wall-clock receipts from the release rig, all on Flash-Next Bare Speed: OpenCode built the complete five-file game in about 40 s per run with mid-session first tokens at 0.11 s; Pi fixed a two-bug module and wrote passing tests in 13.2 s; Hermes did the same class of task in 39.8 s with its 17.7k-token system prompt served from the session bank at 0.3 to 0.5 s first token on every turn after the first.

## The engine plans memory for your Mac

MTPLX no longer assumes a 128 GB studio machine (issue #305 and friends). At startup the serve banner prints the machine plan: engine budget, weights, the resolved context window, and the session bank budget. The default context window is the largest one whose full-window KV actually fits. A 48 GB Mac serving the Speed model resolves 196,608 tokens instead of a physically impossible 262,144, with a loud note instead of a silent swap death. 128 GB machines are unchanged.

- The session bank yields dynamically as a long-context request's KV grows, ahead of any swap. macOS pressure plus an earlier allocator-relative signal drive the shedding guard, and the dynamic ceiling is enforced on every guard tick.
- Metal allocation failures shed caches and answer a structured HTTP 507 instead of crashing the daemon and leaving the next request to hit the same wall (issue #348 class).
- Long agent sessions above the RAM cap spill to SSD tensor by tensor with bounded memory (#305, #323), so a 100k-token coding session survives a restart without a full re-prefill. Spill refusals for disk headroom print one line naming the entry size, the cap, and free disk; nothing is skipped silently.
- `/health`, the dashboard, and the app carry the plan, guard events, and a pressure banner that names the exact `--context-window` fix when overcommit is known. The dashboard's RAM session cache settings report the real resolved budgets instead of inventing 8G and 4G.
- `MTPLX_MEMORY_BUDGET` (or `--memory-budget 48G`) reproduces any seat exactly, including bare suffixes like `48G`, and the 48 GB seat is pinned by integration tests. On that simulated seat at 42k context the release gate measured 33 tok/s decode with zero calls into the slow chunked attention fallback that 2.9.2 users had been living on.

## Long-context decode past 128k, improved

Past 131,072 prompt tokens the engine silently repaged dense decode into a cache layout that structurally excluded the packed verify kernel. Verify fell onto a lazy fallback paying roughly 30 times its normal round cost, and speculative decode collapsed to plain AR speed. On an M5 Max 128 GB at 147k context, decode measured 12.0 tok/s before the fix and 16.3 after, 18.4 with prompt-copy speculation off.

- The dense-decode ceiling is now memory-aware: `MTPLX_SUSTAINED_DENSE_DECODE_MAX_CONTEXT=auto` budgets 15 percent of device RAM against the model's KV bytes per token, clamps to the model's context window, and floors at the old 131,072 literal so no smaller machine regresses.
- The resolution is announced once in the serve log, including whether the model supplied its own KV geometry (`MTPLX_DENSE_KV_BYTES_PER_TOKEN`) or fell back to the Qwen 3.8 default.
- Operators can still pin any numeric ceiling, and an exported env now beats the profile instead of being silently stomped.
- The packed split-KV verify kernel's q_len window extends from 4 to 8 by default, so deeper verify windows keep the fast kernel.

## Long answers and rewrites hold their speed

An uncapped chat answer used to get slower the longer it ran. A 34k-token Flash-Next generation decayed from 86 to 25 tok/s inside one request, and a follow-up that rewrote a file the model had just produced ran at plain speculative depth even though every token of it already sat in the prompt. Per-round attribution put the growth in the graph sync (29.5 ms to 50.0 ms per round across one 12k-token generation) with healthy acceptance throughout. Three causes, all fixed:

- The draft head's committed history cache grew one row per generated token and was never trimmed during decode; the windowing that exists for long prompts keyed off prompt length only. The cache now resets and regrows once a generation appends 16,384 tokens (`MTPLX_MTP_HISTORY_LIVE_RESET_THRESHOLD`, 0 disables). It is keyed on live appends, so a restored long session's seed is never dropped at turn start. Draft conditioning affects acceptance only; the output stream is unchanged by construction, and the cycle-policy alternative was measured strictly worse (34 tok/s with acceptance collapsed to 0.60/0.27/0.10).
- The allocator clear-cache cadence read the prefill-time context, so a short prompt with a long answer never cleared once and the MLX allocator cache grew to 8.6 GB inside a single request. The cadence now arms mid-generation when the live total crosses the same threshold; with it armed the same workload holds 0.6 GB.
- Context-copy block rounds now run on the batched verify lane, the lane Flash-Next uses. When the tail of the stream matches an n-gram in the prompt, the prompt continuation is proposed as a block of up to 24 tokens and verified in one forward, with the same point-mass probability-ratio acceptance as the MTP path, so sampling behavior is identical at any temperature. On the two-turn rewrite scenario the fix targets, the rewrite turn decoded at 87.6 tok/s against 73.8 for the fresh build turn, with 177 block rounds supplying 2,400 of the turn's 3,963 tokens; a fresh build turn proposes zero blocks. `MTPLX_CONTEXT_COPY_BATCHED=0` disables it.

Operator observability rode along: receipts carry the copy-lane counters and, on `MTPLX_DROP_EVENTS=0` launches, a per-round `round_timing_ms` series for decay attribution, and the growth-lever envs (clear-cache cadence, history window and live reset, verify snapshot, family capture, drop-events) now beat the profile instead of being silently stomped.

## Community lanes, adapted with credit

Four community pull requests landed this cycle, each adapted onto the 2.10.0 tree and measured before shipping:

- The warm ladder now yields to live and queued requests and never stamps the traffic clock, so your first message after starting the engine is not sharing the GPU with a warm-up rung (PR #300 by @Blakeolson21).
- Margin-gate and adaptive-depth speculative lanes read the greedy draft token and its confidence in one GPU synchronization instead of two (PR #288 by @ArthurOstapenko). Default lanes are untouched.
- RAMP, an opt-in long-block plus fuzzy re-anchor policy for the context-copy proposer, built for temperature-0 edit-shaped agent runs where its author measured up to +54 percent decode (PR #375 by @johninthewinter). It stays off by default: our temperature-1 chat measurements favor the stock ladder, and off is byte-identical.
- A QSA rows-gather attention lane for verify widths, adapted from the per-query gather and head-group broadcast in PR #380 by @maceip and on by default for Flash-Next past 16k context: multi-row rounds read each row's selected blocks instead of the full KV, which held verify cost flat across a 16k-token answer where the dense path decayed (last-window 64.5 vs 36.0 tok/s). MTPLX_QSA_GATHER=0 turns it off.

## Faster stack: mlx floor raised to 0.32.2

A clean same-wheel A/B on an M5 Max measured mlx 0.32.0 to 0.32.2 at plus 29 percent decode and plus 41 percent prefill at 88k context, and plus 31 percent at 16k. The dependency floor moves to `mlx>=0.32.2` so existing installs converge on the faster stack instead of sitting on 0.32.0 forever. New pin-guard tests fail loudly if an environment or a shipped profile ever drifts below the receipted stack.

## KV cache quantization: from broken to usable

q8 and q4 KV quantization was effectively broken. Turning it on crashed serving at warmup, and before that bug existed it could cost half your decode speed. This release replaces the lane:

- The warmup crash is fixed.
- q4 routes through the exact packed-quant kernel via a persistent quantized bank instead of re-dequantizing the entire prefix every round.
- The compiled verify bank promotes quantized paged caches instead of dropping every verify round to eager execution.
- A pre-existing q4 numerics defect at head_dim 128 is fenced fail-closed; the shipped model family (head_dim 256) is exact.

Measured cost at 16k context on an M5 Max, versus quantization off: q8 about minus 4 percent decode, q4 about minus 19 percent, down from crash or minus 50 percent. That is not yet the zero-loss bar we hold ourselves to, so the toggles stay opt-in: q8 is a fair trade today for double the context headroom, q4 is for capacity emergencies. One known limit: past the dense-decode ceiling, quantized decode still drops onto a slow routing lane. The cause is located and the fix is planned for a follow-up release.

## Session cache and time to first token

- SSD session-cache writes no longer starve on an idle server (#290). An idle pump persists pending sessions within seconds of the server going request-idle (`MTPLX_SSD_IDLE_PUMP`, default on), foreground work always wins, and a SIGTERM or Ctrl-C gives pending writes a bounded flush (default 10 s, `MTPLX_SHUTDOWN_SSD_FLUSH_S`). A killed daemon stops costing a full re-prefill of sessions that were already computed.
- Preserved reasoning history (see the Flash-Next section) keeps agent histories append-only, which is exactly what the session bank needs to commit each turn's state without re-prefilling it.

## See what the engine actually did

The decode cliff hid for months because fast lanes declined silently. That class of blindness is closed:

- Every request row records packed-route bail counters and paged-adapter engagement, so a lane that stops engaging shows up in the request log instead of only in a benchmark weeks later.
- `MTPLX_ROUTE_DEBUG=1` prints one line per layer naming the attention branch taken and every gate input, with a capacity so warmup cannot consume the budget.
- Warmup failures log their full traceback instead of a one-line skip.
- Capture-commit refusals announce their reason (first three per process). A silent fallback had hidden a battery of re-forwards.
- The n-gram staging path carries always-on engagement counters, and TensorOffset paged adapters are counted in the attention engagement stats.
- Memory guard actions ride a ring buffer into the dashboard snapshot.

## Server and API

- Long prefills no longer look like a dead connection (#358). Streaming endpoints emit a `: keep-alive` SSE comment every 5 seconds until the first token, so strict client and proxy read timeouts (Claude Code, Cursor, Open WebUI, nginx, cloudflared) stop dropping healthy requests mid-compute. Off with `MTPLX_SSE_HEARTBEAT=0`, interval tunable with `MTPLX_SSE_HEARTBEAT_INTERVAL_S`.
- The Anthropic bridge (`/v1/messages`) forwards the flat `reasoning_effort` field for every family. The bridge lane had been effort-blind.
- `/health` reports family-true sampler defaults, and `/health` plus the dashboard snapshot carry the memory plan, pressure level, and guard events.
- Constructable models always run. MTP is an accelerator, never a gate: a checkpoint this build can construct but has no MTP or no speculative backend for serves autoregressive with an honest "mtp_off: serving autoregressive" note instead of being refused with a false "MTPLX requires an MTP-equipped model". Refusals are reserved for genuine capability gaps.
- The app-capabilities endpoint advertises the scheduler modes the app may offer; the experimental hyper chassis stays operator-only.

## App

- Each conversation streams on its own turn stream (#324). Switching conversations mid-generation no longer blanks the partial answer or loses the reply.
- Explicit performance settings are honored on every serving target (#325), and the native Chat launch target stops silently ignoring "Handle multiple at once".
- The app accepts custom Hugging Face models the engine reports as runnable (#359). Install completeness is judged by the source repo's own manifest, not by the presence of MTPLX branding, and the failure banner names what is actually missing.
- First-run onboarding lists both Flash-Next packs on machines that fit them, detects already-downloaded packs, and the setup step now verifies the terminal command through the user's login shell. The app's own PATH was the wrong oracle: setup could certify the terminal as up to date while the user's actual shell still ran an older Homebrew install.
- The inference settings panel shows the family's real sampler when the engine is stopped. The offline fallback used to show a generic temperature 0.6 and the label "Custom model" for Flash-Next; it now shows the family's native 1.0 and names the model line. With the engine running the panel always reflected live server truth.
- The update dialog's release notes are readable in dark mode (#367), this page included, and the release script and rehearsal kit render through one shared template so the rehearsal equals the shipped page.
- The Memory Detail card attributes the n-gram table only when it is actually resident.
- The Live tab's acceptance panel shows the finished request's per-depth counters even when a stream frame was missed. The daemon's idle warm ladder no longer replaces the last real request's receipt in the dashboard, and the app now also counts completions it sees through the snapshot poller, so the panel lights within a second of the request finishing instead of waiting for the next stream event that might never come.

## CLI

- `mtplx run` and `mtplx chat` gain `--reasoning-effort` with server-identical resolution.
- `mtplx connect opencode` actually writes the OpenCode config it prints (the model id was previously print-only, which surfaced as "Unexpected server error" in OpenCode).
- `mtplx serve` launched from a directory containing an `mtplx/` folder (a source checkout, a vendored copy) silently ran that folder's code instead of the installed release. Every daemon and tool child now starts Python with `-P`, so the launch directory can never shadow the runtime.
- The model-not-cached hint names the real command, `mtplx pull <model>` (it used to suggest a `--download` flag that does not exist).
- `mtplx inspect` and the registry gate classify official Nemotron-H configs the same way the runtime routes them, so inspect and serve cannot disagree about whether a model runs (#341).

## Fixed

- Forge routes official NVIDIA Nemotron-H configs correctly by deriving the MTP pattern from `mtp_layers_block_type` (#341); load no longer crashes.
- The dynamic-offset paged verify kernel had never compiled since its introduction (a pointer-cast Metal bug hidden behind its own mask gate) and crashed at q_len above 5 once compiled; both are fixed. The kernel stays behind its opt-in gate pending a serve verdict, but the crash class is gone.
- A model that ships without KV-geometry metadata shows itself in the serve log instead of silently mis-budgeting dense decode.
- QSA cache state joins the cache contract, fixing an OpenCode crash at 3.7k context during the family bring-up.
- Family-name collisions are fenced: Qwen4-preview pack names can never claim the dense-27B serving contract, and trunk-only or partial downloads classify as the family instead of an anonymous fallback.
- Serving profiles allowlist the family lane env keys, so a Flash-Next serve cannot boot into an unsupported-key error.
- The public Qwen 3.8 draft-depth cap stays at 3. Depths 4 through 8 were tested and lost; an experimental raise was reverted before ship.

## Experimental, off by default

Gated lanes for operators. Everything here defaults off and changes nothing unless exported.

- `--scheduler-mode hyper`: the single-user manufactured-concurrency chassis (H0). Trajectory-identical to serial in every gate pair.
- `MTPLX_NAX_TILE_ROUTE` (with `MTPLX_NAX_TILE_BLOCKS`): the first M5 tensor-unit attention kernel at decode shapes, plus 34 to plus 45 percent over the scalar wide-QL kernel at QL9 in gated walks.
- `MTPLX_GQA_PACKED_WIDE`: wide-QL packed verify routing for q_len 5 to 16.
- `MTPLX_ADAPTIVE_DTEMP`: acceptance-EMA adaptive draft-temperature controller.
- `MTPLX_CCOPY_BANK_ROUTE` (with `MTPLX_CCOPY_PROF`): copy-speculation dispatch through the compiled verify bank, and its profiler.
- `MTPLX_PAGED_TAILMASK_ELIDE`: the resurrected paged kernel's mask-elide fast path.
- `MTPLX_FORKEV_TELEMETRY`: shadow pricing for speculative fork expected value.
- `MTPLX_DELTA_TELEMETRY`: correction-rank telemetry on every rejection.
- `MTPLX_FRSPEC_DRAFT` and friends: pruned draft-vocabulary prototype.
- `MTPLX_QSA_FLASH`: block-sparse flash-skip lane for Qwen Sparse Attention decode; long-context verdict pending.
- `MTPLX_DEFER_REPAIR_EVAL`: lazy post-rejection repair forward.
- `MTPLX_NGRAM_RESIDENT=1`: wire the full n-gram table in RAM (a bench pin for 160 GB and larger machines; the streamed default meets or beats it in the product register).
- `MTPLX_FUSED_MOE_DECODE`, `MTPLX_FUSED_GDN_OUT`, `MTPLX_FUSED_QSA_QKV`, `MTPLX_QSA_GATHER`: wired but measured slower or flat on this silicon; they stay off and exist for other hardware and future re-tests.
